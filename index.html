Why buildings lose their information

Buildings are the most valuable things we own, and almost none of them have an accurate, current record of what they are. The way this has always been done doesn't work. Information about a building gets produced in bursts — for a permit, for a sale — and then it goes stale, gets contradicted on site, or simply disappears. Everyone downstream pays for that, forever.

◷

Drawings are incomplete and out of date

A set is drawn for a purpose and a date. Half of what matters was never drawn at all, and the day something changes on site the whole set is quietly wrong — but it stays in the folder, and people keep building from it.

⚠

What gets built isn't what was drawn

No building is executed 100% to the drawings. Routes move, dimensions shift, details get solved on the spot. As-builts are the deliverable that most often never arrives — so the gap between paper and reality is never closed.

◱

Renovations are rarely documented

Walls come down, services get rerouted, a bathroom is rebuilt — and nothing is recorded. Twenty years of changes leave no trace, so the next person to touch the building starts by guessing, or by surveying it all over again.

◇

The visuals are made to sell, then thrown away

Floor plans, 3D visualisations, photorealistic renderings — commissioned around a transaction, used for a few months of marketing, then gone. Real money spent on assets that were never connected to the building and were never meant to last.

And the bill lands somewhere else entirely. Bad building information is not an admin problem, it's a cost:
  • Site visits that exist only to measure something that should already be known
  • Something ordered, delivered and then found not to fit
  • Too much material bought and scrapped, or too little and the job stops
  • Surveys paid for twice because nobody kept the first one
  • Decisions made from a drawing that stopped being true years ago

Skanna is a different approach to the whole thing. One accurate digital twin per building, made once from whatever you already have, kept current as the building changes, and handed on with the property when it sells. Drawings, visuals, quantities and operations all come out of that single model instead of being commissioned separately and going out of date separately. That is how the waste disappears — and it only works if the record is accurate, live, and in one place. That is what this platform is for.

How a building gets there

Four steps, once per building — then it stays alive. We recommend starting with the Skanna scanner: it was developed specifically to capture building sites, not objects or faces, so it gives you true-to-scale geometry, photos and 360° panoramas in one walk-through, and it is by a distance the fastest and most accurate way in. No iPhone, no 3D model and no drawings to hand? It still works — trace a floor-plan image, an SVG or a DXF, or draw the building from scratch with a tape measure. Everything above is an output of the same model.

Walk the building with the scanner and capture the geometry, photos, panoramas and notes in one pass — or, without a scanner, bring the drawing, model or measurements you have. Everything lands on the building's page together.

STEP 3

Publish

Drawing sets on your title block, and the twin on a link. Send either to a buyer, a contractor, a lender or the permit office.

STEP 4

Operate

The model stays alive next to the building — inventory, condition, service history, bookings and renovation progress on the objects — and transfers to the next owner when the property does.

Lighting

Every light fixture in the model is a real emitter — spotlights, ceiling lights, pendants, wall sconces and LED strips, each listed and each with its own switch. Throw them all on or off at once, and set the colour temperature anywhere from warm to daylight. The view at the top of this section is the same building with the fixtures on.

Materials the viewer can change

Click any wall, floor or ceiling and swap what it's made of — painted, wood, tile, stone, brick or concrete — then pick a tone. The change is applied to that surface alone, and one control resets it while another resets everything at once. Whatever they change belongs to them: the published model is never touched. Where you'd rather not leave the whole palette open, you can group the surfaces that change together and publish only the named options you're willing to supply — and one choice then restyles every surface in the group.

A floor slab switched to ash wood

Furniture, appliances and equipment

Furnish the unit from a catalogue of real products at their real dimensions — named manufacturer pieces alongside generic joinery and white goods, every one listed with its footprint in centimetres. Pick an item and the twin tells you what to do with it: click a floor to drop it, or a wall to stand it against, turning it in 45° steps as you go. What they place is theirs alone — your published model is never altered, and one button clears everything they added. Publish the full catalogue, or curate it down to the pieces and sizes you're prepared to supply.
